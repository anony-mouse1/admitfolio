I watched people through security cameras, learning how small choices shape a community.

It started with a monitor bank in a strip-mall office: eleven screens, one folding chair, and a summer job nobody else wanted. My shifts were quiet. The footage never was.

People returned carts they could have abandoned. They held doors for strangers they would never see again. Somewhere around week three, the job stopped being about security and started being about attention.

The night manager called it wasted attention. I logged it anyway: timestamps of small kindnesses, a ledger no one asked for. By August it ran nine pages, and I understood the store better than its own floor plan.

I still watch that carefully. Just without the cameras.
